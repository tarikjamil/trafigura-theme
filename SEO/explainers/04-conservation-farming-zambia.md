**Conservation farming in Zambia works when it pays.** COMACO (Community Markets for Conservation) buys crops at premium prices from farmers who use agroforestry and chemical-free methods, sells them under the It’s Wild! brand, and rewards the best performers with conservation dividends. Farmers earn more, land recovers and pressure on wildlife falls.

## Why do poverty and land degradation go together in rural Zambia?

Some 79 percent of people in rural Zambia live below the poverty line, and most of the workforce depends on agriculture. Without reliable markets or fair prices, many farmers turn to slash-and-burn farming, poaching and charcoal production. That leads to deforestation, soil degradation and the loss of Zambia’s natural capital. Erratic rainfall and recurring drought then destroy crops and push farmers further into harmful coping strategies.

## What is COMACO’s model?

[COMACO](/partner-stories/comaco/), a social enterprise founded in 2009, makes conservation the profitable choice:

- **Plans and skills:** it helps communities set up conservation plans and cooperatives and learn to manage their land.
- **Premium markets:** it buys their crops at premium prices and sells them under the It’s Wild! brand.
- **Conservation dividends:** top-performing communities are rewarded.
- **Carbon revenue:** carbon credits bring communities additional income.
- **School feeding:** one of its nutritious products supplies independently managed school feeding programmes.

## What has it achieved?

COMACO has established conservation areas covering 1.7 million hectares in the Luangwa Valley and around Kafue National Park. More than 244 million trees have been planted, improving soil fertility and reducing the need for chemical fertiliser. About 335,000 households have benefited, including 2,380 people who have given up poaching for other trades.

## How does the Trafigura Foundation support COMACO?

The Foundation’s support helps COMACO digitalise its tools and processes and expand into high-biodiversity landscapes in the Lower Zambezi. The partnership goals are:

- Planting another 80 million trees
- Moving 200 illegal hunters into other livelihoods each year
- Installing 15,000 beehives in community forests
- Onboarding 160,000 more households, reaching 2.5 million people with improved resilience to climate change

The work sits across two of the Foundation’s areas of work: [Sustainable livelihoods](/area-of-work/sustainable-livelihoods/) and [Thriving nature](/area-of-work/thriving-nature/).

## Frequently asked questions

**What is agroforestry?**
Growing trees alongside crops. It improves soil fertility, holds water and reduces the need for chemical fertiliser.

**What is It’s Wild!?**
COMACO’s brand for food produced by farmers who follow its conservation practices.

**Where does the Trafigura Foundation work in Zambia?**
