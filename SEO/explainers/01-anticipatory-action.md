**Anticipatory action means releasing funding and help before a predictable disaster hits, not after.** When forecasts show that a flood, drought, heatwave or cyclone is likely, pre-agreed plans and pre-positioned money let local organisations act early: moving people, protecting crops and livestock, and getting supplies in place. It turns a predictable emergency into a manageable one.

## Why does humanitarian aid need to change?

Extreme weather is becoming more frequent and more severe, and it hits hardest in communities that lack the resources to protect their lives and livelihoods. One in 23 people on the planet now needs humanitarian assistance, twice as many as four years ago.

Billions of dollars in humanitarian aid are spent every year to help people recover. But little of that money is organised in advance, even though planning ahead is a more efficient and effective way to help communities cope. The aid system is still largely reactive, fragmented and slow.

## How does anticipatory action work in practice?

It rests on three things agreed before a crisis:

1. **A forecast or danger threshold** (for example, a rainfall deficit that signals drought).
2. **A contingency plan** that sets out who does what when the threshold is reached.
3. **Pre-positioned funding** that is released automatically when the threshold is crossed.

[Start Ready](/partner-stories/start-network-start-ready/), a pooled humanitarian fund run by Start Network, a global network of non-governmental organisations, works exactly this way. It pre-positions funding for recurring, predictable crises such as floods, droughts, heatwaves and cyclones. Pooling funds and risks across countries lets it cover more hazards in more places.

## Is there still a role for rapid response?

Yes. Not every crisis can be forecast. The [Start Fund](/partner-stories/start-network-start-fund/) is one of the fastest-acting emergency response mechanisms in the world, releasing funds within 72 hours of a crisis. Nearly two-thirds of the alerts raised by its members are climate-related. To date it has responded to more than 700 crises, disbursed more than USD 220 million and reached 29 million people in more than 80 countries.

Anticipatory action and rapid response work best together: plan for what can be predicted, and respond fast to what cannot.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the difference between anticipatory action and disaster response?**
Disaster response starts after a disaster hits. Anticipatory action starts when a forecast shows a disaster is likely, using plans and funding agreed in advance.

**Which hazards suit anticipatory action?**
Recurring, predictable ones: floods, droughts, heatwaves and cyclones.

**Who funds anticipatory action?**
Humanitarian donors, governments and philanthropic foundations, often through pooled funds such as Start Ready. The Trafigura Foundation funds Start Ready and the IRC’s Anticipatory Action Fund.
